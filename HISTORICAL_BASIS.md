# Historical basis for ship provisioning

This feature uses historical ships and documented ranges as anchors, then states its gameplay abstractions explicitly. Early-modern tonnage was a capacity measure, not a standardized modern mass measurement; Elcano labels it **tons burden** and treats it as shared hold space.

## Vessel profiles

| Elcano preset | Historical anchor | Capacity and complement used |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lateen vessel | Fifteenth-century Portuguese lateen caravel | 50 tons burden; 24-person gameplay cap. Typical caravels are commonly estimated at 50–60 tons; the complement is a cautious gameplay approximation. |
| Caravel | *Santiago* in Magellan’s 1519 fleet | 75 toneles (90 toneladas); 31 people. |
| Nao / carrack | *Victoria* in Magellan–Elcano’s 1519 fleet | 85 toneles; 44-person cap. Sources describe an initial crew of about 42–44. |
| Galleon | Sixteenth-/seventeenth-century Spanish Atlantic galleon | 500 tons and 150 people: the low end of a documented 500–1,200 ton range with up to 150 people aboard. |
| Square-rigged vessel | Early-sixteenth-century Iberian zabra | 60 tons and 30 people: a large representative of the documented 20–60 ton range. |

## Consumption and pay

- Water uses a compact 3 litres (0.003 tons) per person per day.
- Provisions use 1.5 kg (0.0015 tons) per person per day. This matches the scale of later documented Spanish treasure-fleet bread rations, while standing in for the wider period diet of biscuit, legumes, salted meat/fish, oil and wine.
- Wages are daily game deductions in maravedís. Sailors cost 40 and soldiers 50 per day. This preserves Elcano’s intended sailor/soldier trade-off; it is not presented as a universal historical pay table. Historical pay varied sharply by rank, speciality, place and period.
- Gold occupies hold capacity but is converted into a compact treasury of 100,000 maravedís per ton for gameplay. This conversion is deliberately fictional, not a historical price claim.
